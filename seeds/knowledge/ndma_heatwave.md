# NDMA National Guidelines: Heatwave Safety

## Do's During Extreme Heat (Source: IMD-NDMA Guidelines, Page 1)
- Listen to Radio, watch TV, read newspaper for local weather news or download weather information mobile apps.
- Drink sufficient water and stay hydrated even if not thirsty. Persons on fluid-restricted diets should consult a doctor before increasing intake.
- Use ORS (Oral Rehydration Solution), homemade drinks like lassi, torani (rice water), lemon water, buttermilk, and coconut water to keep yourself hydrated.
- Wear lightweight, light-coloured, loose, and porous cotton clothes.
- If outside, cover your head: use a cloth, hat, or umbrella. Use sunglasses to protect eyes and sunscreen to protect skin.
- Provide resting shade, clean drinking water, buttermilk, first-aid kits with ice-packs and ORS for all workers at workplaces.
- Schedule strenuous jobs to cooler times of the day; increase frequency and length of rest breaks for outdoor activities.
- In case of heat stroke emergencies, call National Emergency helpline 112 or Disaster helpline 1077.

## Don'ts During Extreme Heat (Source: IMD-NDMA Guidelines, Page 3)
- Avoid going out in direct sun, especially between 12:00 noon and 3:00 PM.
- Avoid strenuous outdoor activities when outside during peak afternoon hours.
- Do not leave children or pets alone inside parked locked vehicles under direct sunlight.
- Avoid cooking during peak hours; open doors and windows to ventilate cooking area adequately.
- Avoid alcohol, tea, coffee, and carbonated soft drinks, which dehydrate the body.
- Avoid high-protein, salty, spicy, and oily food. Do not eat stale food.
- Avoid using incandescent light bulbs and appliances which generate unnecessary heat.

## Treatment of Sunstroke and Vulnerable Groups (Source: IMD-NDMA Guidelines, Page 2-3)
- Special care for vulnerable groups: infants, young children, pregnant women, the elderly, sick or overweight individuals are more likely to become victims of excessive heat.
- Cool indoor spaces with curtains, fans, damp clothing, and frequent cold water baths; keep windows closed during day and open at night.
- Treatment of a person affected by sunstroke: use a wet cloth or pour cool water on the victim's head and neck.
- Give the affected person ORS, lemon water, or torani to drink to rehydrate the body.
- If experiencing persistently high body temperature, throbbing headache, dizziness, weakness, nausea, or disorientation, seek medical attention immediately.
- Call National Emergency helpline 112 or Disaster Management helpline 1077 for urgent medical assistance.
